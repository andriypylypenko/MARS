# 1. What is Capability?

## 1.1 Capability Definition

Capability within MARS represents explicitly permitted operational ability to perform bounded actions within organizational system context.

Capabilities define:
- permitted actions;
- operational boundaries;
- governance applicability;
- disclosure permissions;
- reconstruction permissions;
- interpretive permissions;
- escalation permissions.

Capabilities do not themselves constitute Authority.

Possession of Capability means only that:
- system architecture;
- governance policy;
- operational Constraints

permit bounded execution of specific actions.

Capabilities may apply to:
- Human Controllers;
- delegated human operators;
- AI-assisted mechanisms;
- deterministic system processes;
- disclosure mechanisms;
- reconstruction mechanisms.

Capabilities within MARS follow default-deny semantics.

Actions not explicitly permitted must be considered prohibited.

---

# 2. Authority vs Capability

## 2.1 Authority Definition

Authority represents governance-recognized permission to perform organizational action.

Authority is established through:
- system architecture;
- governance structures;
- operational policy;
- Rulesets;
- delegated organizational responsibility;
- explicit authorization semantics.

---

## 2.2 Capability Definition

Capability represents practical ability to perform operational action within system context.

Capability may exist:
- with Authority;
- without Authority;
- temporarily;
- accidentally;
- maliciously;
- through architectural failure.

Possession of Capability does not imply legitimacy of action.

---

## 2.3 Governance Principle

MARS must distinguish between:
- authorized action;
- merely possible action.

System architecture should minimize existence of Capabilities not supported by explicit Authority.

Governance within MARS prioritizes:
- explicit authorization;
- bounded operational Capability;
- deterministic permission boundaries;
- minimization of unauthorized operational Capability.

---

# 3. Human Governance Hierarchy

## 3.1 Human Controller Authority

MARS governance is centered around Human Controller authority.

Human Controllers retain ultimate governance Authority over:
- authoritative fact acceptance;
- Ruleset authorization;
- disclosure authorization;
- conflict resolution;
- escalation handling;
- operational governance decisions.

---

## 3.2 Delegation

Delegation represents temporary bounded grant of Authority to actors not normally possessing it.

Delegation must remain:
- explicit;
- attributable;
- time-bounded;
- operationally scoped;
- governance-reconstructable.

Delegated Authority must define:
- permitted actions;
- operational boundaries;
- validity period;
- disclosure limitations;
- escalation requirements.

---

## 3.3 Override Authority

Override Authority represents ability to prioritize instructions or governance decisions of one actor over actions, processes or decisions of others.

Override Authority may apply to:
- Workflow execution;
- replay operations;
- disclosure restrictions;
- escalation handling;
- interpretive applicability;
- operational prioritization.

Override actions must remain:
- attributable;
- reconstructable;
- governance-visible.

---

## 3.4 Emergency Authority

Emergency Authority represents temporary exceptional Delegation activated under extraordinary operational conditions.

Emergency Authority remains subject to:
- attribution;
- bounded scope;
- temporal limitation;
- governance reconstruction;
- post-event review.

Emergency Authority does not suspend:
- authoritative basis integrity;
- replay traceability;
- governance reconstruction requirements.

---

## 3.5 Escalation

Escalation represents transfer of unresolved ambiguity,
conflict or uncertainty to Human Controller review.

Escalation may occur where AI-assisted mechanisms cannot reliably resolve:
- contradictory assertions;
- disclosure ambiguity;
- governance conflict;
- insufficient validation basis;
- reconstruction inconsistency;
- interpretive uncertainty.

Escalation must remain:
- attributable;
- reconstructable;
- governance-visible.

---

# 4. AI Capability Boundaries

## 4.1 Permitted AI Activities

AI-assisted mechanisms within MARS exist under explicitly bounded operational Constraints.

AI systems may assist:
- OCR;
- extraction;
- classification;
- ambiguity detection;
- anomaly identification;
- interpretive assistance;
- reconstruction assistance;
- replay execution;
- analytical rendering;
- escalation triggering;
- Workflow assistance.

AI-assisted mechanisms must remain subordinate to:
- Human governance Authority;
- deterministic Rulesets;
- explicit Capability boundaries;
- disclosure restrictions;
- authoritative basis integrity.

---

## 4.2 Forbidden AI Actions

AI-assisted mechanisms must not independently perform actions capable of irreversibly or authoritatively affecting organizational basis.

Forbidden AI actions include:
- treasury execution;
- payment authorization;
- bank transaction execution;
- asset disposal authorization;
- liability creation authorization;
- contractual commitment authorization;
- governance Authority delegation;
- Ruleset authorization;
- authoritative disclosure authorization;
- unrestricted information dissemination;
- destructive modification of authoritative basis;
- destructive deletion of Events;
- irreversible replay suppression;
- authoritative conflict arbitration;
- override authorization;
- emergency governance activation;
- external legally binding commitments;
- independent Authority generation.

AI-assisted mechanisms must not independently:
- create authoritative organizational Obligations;
- alter governance hierarchy;
- authorize economically significant actions;
- suppress ambiguity requiring escalation;
- fabricate certainty from insufficient basis.

---

## 4.3 AI Operational Principle

AI within MARS operates under principle:

Perform only explicitly permitted bounded actions.

Capabilities not explicitly granted must remain prohibited.

System architecture should minimize existence of:
- unintended operational Capability;
- implicit Authority;
- unrestricted disclosure Capability;
- unauthorized basis mutation Capability.

# 5. Capability Grant Semantics

## 5.1 Explicit Capability Grant

Capabilities within MARS follow explicit-allow and default-deny principles.

Actions not explicitly permitted must remain prohibited.

Capabilities must be:
- explicitly granted;
- operationally scoped;
- governance-attributable;
- reconstructable.

Capability grants must define:
- permitted actions;
- operational boundaries;
- disclosure permissions;
- temporal applicability;
- escalation requirements.

---

## 5.2 Default-Deny Principle

MARS operates under default-deny Capability semantics.

Absence of explicit Capability grant must be interpreted as prohibition.

Default-deny semantics apply to:
- Human actors;
- AI-assisted mechanisms;
- Workflows;
- disclosure mechanisms;
- replay operations;
- interpretive operations.

---

## 5.3 Temporary Capability Grants

Capabilities may be granted temporarily.

Temporary grants must define:
- validity period;
- operational scope;
- governance Constraints;
- disclosure restrictions;
- revocation conditions.

Expired temporary Capabilities must automatically lose operational validity.

---

## 5.4 Revocability

Capabilities must remain revocable through governance-authorized action.

Revocation must remain:
- attributable;
- reconstructable;
- governance-visible.

Capability revocation must not destructively alter:
- historical governance reconstruction;
- authoritative operational history;
- prior attributable actions.

---

## 5.5 Non-Inheritance

Capabilities are non-inheritable unless explicitly delegated.

Possession of Capability by one actor, process or mechanism must not implicitly grant Capability to others.

Delegated Capabilities must remain:
- explicit;
- bounded;
- reconstructable;
- governance-traceable.

---

# 6. Disclosure Authority

## 6.1 Disclosure Governance Principle

Disclosure within MARS exists under explicit governance control.

Possession of information within MARS does not constitute authorization for disclosure.

Disclosure permissions must remain:
- explicit;
- attributable;
- reconstructable;
- governance-bounded.

---

## 6.2 Recipient Authorization

Recipients of organizational information must possess explicit authorization to receive disclosed information.

Disclosure authorization may depend upon:
- organizational role;
- governance Authority;
- operational necessity;
- disclosure classification;
- confidentiality Constraints.

Information not explicitly authorized for disclosure must remain isolated.

---

## 6.3 Disclosure Isolation

MARS must isolate all organizational information beyond explicitly permitted disclosure scope.

Isolation boundaries apply to:
- Human actors;
- AI-assisted mechanisms;
- replay operations;
- analytical rendering;
- reporting mechanisms;
- communication channels.

System architecture should minimize:
- unrestricted visibility;
- implicit disclosure Capability;
- cross-context information leakage;
- unauthorized interpretive exposure.

---

## 6.4 Reports as Detached Disclosure Artifacts

Reports represent detached disclosure artifacts generated from reconstructed representations.

Reports should not maintain direct live linkage to authoritative basis following authorized disclosure generation.

Disclosure artifacts therefore represent:
- bounded outputs;
- governance-approved representations;
- isolated disclosure snapshots.

---

## 6.5 Deterministic Disclosure Perimeter

Disclosure within MARS operates under deterministic disclosure perimeter principles.

Disclosure mechanisms must ensure:
- only explicitly authorized information is disclosed;
- recipients remain explicitly identifiable;
- disclosure boundaries remain reconstructable;
- unauthorized disclosure Capability remains minimized.

Disclosure mechanisms should prefer:
- bounded deterministic delivery;
- explicit recipient mapping;
- governance-controlled output generation;
- minimal disclosure surface area.

---

# 10. Governance Failure Semantics

## 10.1 Critical Governance Failures

Certain governance failures within MARS constitute critical operational failure conditions.

Critical governance failures may include:
- missing required Rulesets;
- absence of Human Controller Authority;
- unresolved authoritative ambiguity;
- unresolved governance conflict;
- unreconstructable disclosure authorization;
- critical replay inconsistency.

---

## 10.2 Missing Rulesets

Required Rulesets must normally remain installed and reconstructable.

Absence of required Rulesets constitutes critical reconstruction failure.

MARS must not silently substitute missing Rulesets or fabricate Interpretations under insufficient reconstruction conditions.

---

## 10.3 Absence of Human Governance Authority

Absence of Human Controller governance Authority constitutes governance failure condition.

Where Human Controller Authority is unavailable:
- escalation may propagate to upper organizational governance structures;
- authoritative conflict resolution may be suspended;
- disclosure authorization may be restricted;
- interpretive rendering may be limited.

---

## 10.4 Unresolved Ambiguity

Where authoritative ambiguity cannot be deterministically resolved,
MARS may refuse reconstruction or interpretive rendering.

MARS must not silently fabricate certainty under unresolved governance ambiguity.

---

## 10.5 Governance Escalation Under Failure

Governance failures and unresolved ambiguity must trigger escalation where possible.

Escalation targets may include:
- Human Controllers;
- delegated governance Authority;
- upper organizational governance structures.

Governance failure handling must remain:
- attributable;
- reconstructable;
- governance-visible.

---
